Hot-bed made of glass

The original hot-bed is made of aluminum, and that's a problem because it is a material who dilates and deform widley with constant temperature changes, besides the scratches caused by the extruder if not properly align.

One of the most popular moddifications is to put a pice of glass, given that it offers a surface non-porous, rigid, with good thermal conductivity and proper adhesion if previously coated with hairspray or school glue as intermediary material.

For that I glued a pice of glass of the same proportions of the hot-bed with silicon.

Infrared sensor

Here I added another sensor wich maps nanometric differences in different points of the surface of impression.

To achive this an adapter for the sensor was created (with the printer itself) and the firmware was modified to use said information to perform mild modifications of the Z axis (altitude) during the printing of a layer (beforehand altitude was only changed at the time of start a new layer)

As a result the percentage of success on the correct printing and adhesion of the model increased aproximately from a 20-40% (depending on maintenance status) to a 70-90%, and also it reduced the need of manual adjustments and maintenance tasks on a third of the time previously invested.
